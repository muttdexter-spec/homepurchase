Written 23 September 2026, after the QA pass of the same day. Read this with no session context.

not yet committed: GitHub and the live page still carry the 16 September build (40 listings), so

## The short answer

**No pass over all 58.** The 40 older houses were read to the written photo protocol (median 13
of 15 surfaces) and half of them may no longer be for sale. The weaknesses sit in three places,
and each has a narrow fix:

1. **Status.** 21 of the 40 older houses were missing from both 23 September searches, including
   **8 of the current top 10** (the QA note says four). Every other kind of work is wasted on a
   house that has sold.
2. **The 18 new houses were read at about half depth.** An unread surface falls back to the
   pessimistic era prior, so the new houses are ranked against the old ones on unequal evidence.
   If the missing houses are gone, five of the live top ten are new houses, none read above
   medium evidence.
3. **One scoring rule lets good news lower a score.** A clean read of the windows or the panel
   deletes a line from the condition denominator. On ten houses a perfectly clean read would lower
   condition today. This has to be fixed before any re-read or showing record goes in, or the next
   round of evidence moves scores the wrong way.

**Recommended round, in this order:** status of the 21, then the scoring fix, then a protocol
re-read of the six new houses that could reach the live top ten (Thorpe, Wentworth, Sycamore,
Terraview, Parker, Nevarc). About one session. Three things need Alex's yes: the status route
(James or a live check), the scoring fix (it moves scores; no catalog number changes), and opening
the six listing pages.

## 3. One scoring rule lets good news lower a score

`condition = 1 - expected work / possible work`. Both sums run over the lines the house currently
carries, and two kinds of clean read **delete a line** instead of lowering its probability: vinyl
windows (no window line at all) and a modern panel on a pre-1970 house (no panel or rewire line).
When the deleted line's probability was below the house's overall ratio, the ratio gets worse and
condition falls.

- **3148 Terraview**: confirm its windows are vinyl and condition goes **down**, 65.9 to 63.6,
  although expected work falls by $2,700.
- **1493 Clinton**: un-read its vinyl windows and condition goes **up**, 70.6 to 72.2, and it
  passes Seton for third.
- **Ten houses** would score lower after a perfectly clean read of everything still unread:
  Cherrywood, Lark, Weir, Samford, Caplan, Aldridge, Centennial, Rexway, Enfield and Idlewood.

The model already knows this. The docstring of `_resolved_condition()` describes exactly this
artefact on four of those houses, and fixed it for the card's preview line by holding the
denominator still. The score itself was never fixed, so the preview and the score now disagree:

| House | Condition today | Card: "a showing could move this to ... if everything reads clean" | What the model would score that visit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 659 Cherrywood | 76.0 | 79 | 75.3 |
| 395 Lark | 76.4 | 80 | 75.8 |
| 462 Samford | 53.4 | 56 | 52.0 |
| 568 Weir | 50.9 | 54 | 49.4 |

At the other end the held denominator lets expected work exceed it ($202,255 against $199,607), so
Wentworth's card says a bad showing takes condition to **0**; with the denominator allowed to move
it would be about 20.

The grading handoff already states the rule the code breaks: "Nothing gets zeroed on photographic
evidence, ever. Vinyl window frames confirmed at full resolution drop the window provision ... to
about $5k, allowing for a few originals."

**Minimal fix, tested.** A clean read lowers the line to the observed floor (0.15); it never deletes
it. Two places in `cost()`: vinyl windows, and a breaker panel on a pre-1970 or unknown-year house.
No catalog number, era prior or contingency changes.

- Monotone on all 58 in both directions: no clean read lowers condition, no defect read raises it.
- Rank movement today is small: mean 0.9 places, largest 5 (Maderna 41 to 36, Devlin 27 to 23,
  Bonnie 21 to 18, Parker 19 to 22 as others pass it). **Top ten unchanged as a set**; Clinton and
  Seton swap, as do Hazelwood and Thorpe, and Pondview and Columbus.
- Highest condition becomes 79.2, so the "nothing above 80 without a showing" bound still holds.
- Because reserve lines feed upkeep, the kept window line puts about $10 a month back into the
  payment on vinyl-window houses.
- The card's preview can then use the real denominator and say what a showing would actually do:
  Wentworth becomes 62 if clean, 25 if not, instead of 59 and 0.

An alternative was also tested, a fixed worst-case denominator per house. It is monotone too, but
moves ranks more (mean 1.7, largest 7, one top-ten change) and lifts three houses above 80 on photos
alone, which breaks bound 4. Not recommended.

**A related design question for the next model review, not for this round.** `p_hold()` treats a
component of known age much more harshly than one of unknown date. A furnace of unknown date in an
old house counts 25% likely to be replaced in a five-year hold; the same furnace known to be 10
years old counts 50%, and 13 years old 71%. Averaged over an unknown date, the known-age rule would
give about 60%, so the two branches are not consistent with each other. Two consequences: a showing
record that confirms a mid-life component lowers the score (Cherrywood's condition goes from 76.0
to 74.4 if a 2014 roof is recorded), and houses built about 1995 to 2010 have most of their
mechanicals counted as certain to be replaced inside the hold. A standard survival curve (Weibull,
shape 3, mean equal to the catalog life)
moves Columbus 10 to 6 and Wood 39 to 33 and leaves the top ten unchanged. This is Alex's call
and a natural question for the independent review.

## 4. Smaller things found

- **755 Parker, layout.** The size credit (+10, primary over 150 sq ft) comes from the upstairs
  169 sq ft bedroom, and the ensuite credit (+10) from the basement primary suite. The remarks say
  the three upstairs bedrooms share one bath. Read consistently, there is no ensuite on the
  primary, layout goes 65 to 55, and Parker goes 19 to 22.
- **Pie lots.** Lot area is frontage times depth. Seven court, crescent and place lots come out
  under 3,500 sq ft that way; Seton, Ravine and Bonnie (23.8 ft frontage) score lot 0. If they are
  pie-shaped, each is under-measured. At 5,000 sq ft they move one to three places (Bonnie 21 to
  18, Christina 15 to 13). The MLS lot-irregularities line settles it and can be read during page
  loads that happen anyway.
- **1237 Sycamore, bath count.** MLS says 3 full plus 1 half; the room table lists two full baths.
  The remarks describe a newly created ensuite, probably missing from the table. Baths scores 100
  on the count of three. Confirm at the showing; at two full it goes 17 to 19 (baths falls to 75,
  and condition rises 58.9 to 61.7 because one bath job leaves the cost model, the same denominator
  effect as section 3).
- **5190 Wood Crescent.** `primary_bed_sqft` is 192; the room table says 149. The +10 for a primary
  over 150 depends on which is right.
- **`decision.csv`, `score_3` and `score_10`** are computed with the old eight-component formula,
  while `rank_3`, `rank_10` and `score` use the price reframe. The page does not show them, but
  anyone reading the CSV (the independent review included) sees 3 and 10 year scores that do not
  match the ranks beside them: reading down the list, `score_3` rises at 10 places where the rank
  falls. Barberry reads 75.5 at five years and 79.8 at three; on the rank's own scale the three-year
  figure is 76.1.
- **Price** is at its ceiling (payment at or under $6,000) on 38 of 57 houses, so the payment orders
  only 19. That is his anchor and unchanged; noted because this round added houses mostly under
  $6,000.
- **2250 Munn's is a link home.** The model has no attached flag. Whether attached is acceptable at
  all is a yes or no for Alex; if not, it is a gate, not a score.
- **Weights.** Under wide uncertainty around the provisional weights (each free to move roughly
  half to one and a half times its value), the top six stay in the top ten 94 to 100% of the time.
  From 7th to 25th, 0 to 78%. 40 of the 56 adjacent pairs in the list are within one point of each
  other, about what the floor read alone moves a house. The band is the honest reading;
  the fifteen choices remain the biggest lever on the middle of the list.
- **The public page carries a portal token.** The "Open listing" links include a OneHome token
  that base64-decodes to Alex's email address, his agent's portal ID and the saved-search ID. The
  repo and the page are public, and the 16 September build already live has it. Stripping the
  token from the links (or linking by MLS number) would close it.

## 8. How the numbers were produced

- The 23 September files were staged read-only from the laptop clone. `score.py` rerun on them
  reproduced `decision.csv` and `detail.json` by md5 before anything else was done.
- Every scenario is `score.run()` on a modified copy of `observations.json`, with the unchanged
  `costs.yaml`, in a temporary folder. The harness reproduced every rank and score of the committed
  build before any modification.
- Depth scenarios: a surface counts as "photographed but unread" when it is `not_shown` and the
  record's own `photo_inventory` has at least one frame of the matching room. Clean and defect
  values match the model's own `CLEAN_READ` and `DEFECT_READ` (panelling stands in for bare block
  on basement walls; both price the same). Moisture and the panel were left alone: a confirmed
  moisture read is a hard stop, and no listing shows a readable panel. "Typical" draws each such
  surface from the values the 12 to 16 September round recorded for that field, 40 draws per
  house. Ranks in the table hold every other house at today's score.
- Section 3, "ten houses score lower after a clean read": every unseen surface on all 58 resolved
  with the model's `CLEAN_READ`, rescored with `cost()` and `work_expected()` exactly as the build
  does, and compared with today's condition.
- Section 3 fix: implemented as a patched `cost()` in the harness only. Monotonicity was checked by
  resolving every unseen surface clean, then defective, on all 58, with the real denominator. The
  `p_hold()` alternative was a patched `p_hold()` in the same way.
- An independent check, written from scratch without this harness, confirmed the status count,
  the cohort figures, the Terraview and Clinton window results, the list of ten houses, the card
  preview gap, Parker, and the `score_3` finding. It corrected one estimate (Sycamore at two full
  baths goes to 19, not about 22), which is fixed above.
- Weights: 4,000 Dirichlet draws centred on the provisional weights (concentration 30; the 90%
  interval for condition is 21 to 48, for space 10 to 35, for price 6 to 27), each ranking computed
  with the price reframe exactly as `order_under()` does. The unperturbed draw reproduced the
  committed order.
- The analysis scripts were not added to the repo.

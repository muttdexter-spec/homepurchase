## 4. Four things a reviewer should push back on

**4.1 The ensuite is worth 10 layout points and rests on the remarks, not the room table.**
The MLS room table lists only two bathrooms (a 2pc on the main, one 4pc on the second level)
against a stated count of three. The remarks say the primary has a 4-piece ensuite and a walk-in
closet, and three distinct bathrooms do appear in the photographs (frames 19, 23, 28). `ensuite`
is recorded True on that basis. If the showing finds the second-level 4pc is a shared level bath
and the third bathroom is in the basement, layout drops 10 and the house falls out of the teens
into the mid twenties. **Confirm at the showing.**

**4.2 The Living Room dimension in the MLS is wrong and was not silently corrected.**
It is given as 40.67 x 12.3 ft, which is 500 sq ft, more than a quarter of the entire above-grade
area in one room. Almost certainly a typo for 20.67, or a living-plus-dining run entered as one
length. `main_living_sqft` is therefore taken from the Family Room at 133 sq ft, which is what the
photographs support, and the 500 stays in `rooms_raw` so the error remains visible. Neither field
feeds a score, so nothing moves either way.

**4.3 The pool's annual carry is priced. Its capital items are not.**
`pool_carry_annual` ($2k / $3k / $5k, charged every year of the hold) is in the reserve and the
card carries HOLD: POOL. There is **no catalog line at all** for a liner, heater, pump, filter,
coping or safety cover, so none of them is costed. Nine of the 41 photographs are the pool and not
one shows the equipment pad. On a pool that could date from 1974 or from last year, this is the
largest uncosted exposure on the house. It is a gap in the cost model, not a gap in this record.
Left as a flag rather than fixed, because fixing it means a new catalog cost and the standing rule
is that catalog numbers do not change without being asked for.

**4.4 The green space the listing sells earns this house nothing.**
"Backing onto green space" is the headline and the view really is onto a ravine. That strip is
0.5 ha and 122 m away. The `green` part of location counts only parks of 5 ha or more, so the
nearest qualifying park is Brant Hills at 549 m and that is what scores. The rule is working
exactly as written in `location_parts()`; it is simply the one place on this house where the
score and the photographs disagree, and a human looking at the backyard will rate it higher than
the model does.

Also worth knowing, though not a dispute: **the 407 is 527 m north.** That is the nearest noise
source and it is what holds `quiet` at 0.82 instead of full marks. Devlin Drive itself is not an
arterial and takes no cap, but "Drive" is not in `quiet_street_types_calm`, so it takes no calm
bonus either.

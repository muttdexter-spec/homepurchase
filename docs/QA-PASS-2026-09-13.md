## The note

**What changed.** Three things, in order.

1. **Six bugs in the cost model** (`REVIEW-2026-09-12.md` section 5), taken from the tested
   patches in the handoff package. `"inish" in basement` matched "Unfinished", so unfinished
   basements were counted as finished and given an estimated area; "Partially Finished" was
   estimated as a full finish; `"alk"` matched "Walk-Up", so three listings got the walk-out
   weight; a clean floor read counted in full even where the same record said the floor was

   for a furnace twice. No catalog cost, recovery band, era prior or contingency was touched.
   `out/decision.csv` on the fixed model is md5 `3442cb238e38a914ef43dee76036c5cb`, matching
   `reference/decision_v3.4.csv` byte for byte.
2. **The ranking is no longer dollars per usable square foot.** Gates first, then two numbers
   per house: cost to own over the hold in dollars a month, and a facts-only score out of 100
   whose space term saturates and whose condition term can only subtract. The order trades one
   against the other at $120 a month per point of score, which is the buyer's number and the
   only preference in the ranking. Overpay against the dominance frontier is still computed and
   shown, as the argument for paying less rather than as the order. `rank_v5.py` was integrated
   into `score.py` and deleted, so the numbers come from one place, and the V5 config moved into
   `costs.yaml` under `hold`, `gates`, `fit_weights` and `rank`. Specification in
   `ranking-system-v5.md`.
3. **The page says so.** Overpay per month is the headline on every rail, the house that
   dominates this one is named in a line under the address, the score panel gained a Condition
   row that shows the subtraction, the money hero moved to own /mo, a bullet bar draws the
   overpay as a gap, the table swapped `$/sq ft` for `Own/mo` and `Over/mo` and grew three group
   headers, and a hold toggle switches the numbers between 3, 5 and 10 years without re-sorting.
   The ask-derived resale block is no longer rendered on the card. No CSS variable, font size,
   colour or card section order changed; the style blocks are byte-identical to the previous
   build.

## 0. Four changes made after the first review of the built page

Requested by the owner on 13 September, after looking at it.

1. **The three unranked houses were appearing first, not last.** The cause was not the sort. The
   showing-record runtime in the page reorders the card stack on load by the `rank` field in
   `SHOW_DATA`, and it also orders the strip and the jump list by it. Gated houses were being
   given `rank: 0`, which put them at the top of all three. They now carry ranks above 90, so
   they sort last everywhere, and the strip prints "not ranked" rather than a number. While in
   there, two strings in that runtime were still describing the old model: the strip read
   `$620/sf · model rank 16` and its subtitle read "cheapest per usable foot first". They now
   read cost to own per month, which is what the strip is actually sorted on.
2. **The five best-value houses keep numeric ranks.** DISPLAY-SPEC 1.7 and 2.1 put `F` in the
   rank slot for them, so that the page did not assert an order the buyer has not chosen. The
   owner asked for numbers, so they are 1 to 5 by cost to own, and the claim the `F` was
   protecting is now made in words instead: the group header, the glossary and the explainer all
   say that the five are ranked by cost and that choosing among them is a question about what you
   want to spend. This is a deliberate deviation from the display spec.
3. **The grade now sets the order, which is the substantive change in this pass.** The complaint
   was that the ranking made no sense, and it was correct: the sort was `(overpay, own)`, which
   contains no grade term at all, so the top of the list was every cheap C+ in the batch and the
   only A in the set sat at 11. The frontier's five were ordered by cost among themselves, and
   nothing anywhere rewarded a better house. The fix is the exchange rate the review said had to
   be elicited (section 2.9), elicited: the buyer was shown the list under four settings and chose
   $120 a month per fit point. The sort key is now `own_mo - 120 * fit`, so the order reads
   A, A-, A-, A-, B+, B+, B+, B-, B, B down the top ten, with cost to own breaking ties between
   houses that score the same. Two consequences worth naming. **The frontier houses are no longer
   the top five**, because being on the frontier is a statement about price rather than about the
   house; they are stamped best value where they fall, and the page says that this does not put
   them at the top. **The table lost its frontier and dominated groups**, since the two sets are
   no longer contiguous in the order; there is now one ranked block with the rule stated above it,
   and the gated three at the end. Overpay is still computed, still on every card, and is now
   presented as the argument for paying less rather than as the ranking.
4. **A plain-language pass over the grading, scoring and ranking.** The page was accurate and
   hard to read. The howto block became a four-step explainer with the steps on separate lines,
   because as one paragraph nobody would read it. `Frontier` became `Best value` everywhere a
   buyer meets it, with `frontier` kept as the technical name in the glossary and the docs.
   `overpay` became `extra per month, for no more house`. The score panel's heading became
   "how much house" and its footnote now explains what the rows are before explaining the
   colours. The three table group headers became sentences. The rail keeps the same two numbers
   in the same places; only the words under them changed.
8. **The `Robust` stamp in display spec 2.3 was not built.** It needs the sensitivity grid
   re-run inside the build, which is backlog item 13 and out of scope for this pass.
9. **Two review findings were deliberately left undone**, both flagged in the patches
   themselves: windows confirmed original are still annualised over a full life rather than over
   the hold (5.7), and there is no `furnace_seen` or `roof_seen` promotion wired to the reserve
   (5.10, backlog item 10).
10. **`reserve.pool_carry_annual` was added to `costs.yaml` as review 6.2 asks, and is not
    wired to anything.** Wiring it would change the money numbers, which is backlog item 11 and
    was not in scope. It is marked `UNSOURCED` and `NOT WIRED`.

## 4. The seven display conventions inferred for builder parity

No `.py` file in either package contains the string `scorepanel`, so the builder that produced
the deployed page was reconstructed from `reference/page/` and from the deployed pages
themselves. It now reproduces both builds byte for byte on the untouched v3.2 inputs. These seven
rules had to be inferred to get there, and each is now a comment in `build_walkthrough.py`:

1. A `.sp` row is `sp bad` under 30% of its weight, `sp weak` under 50%, else `sp`. Both the
   number and the bar width use `round()`.
2. `sp-foot` names the components filling under half their weight, in component order. One reads
   "here the lot is"; two or more read "here size and layout and lot are".
3. Split-level cards carry a second `sp-foot` line, and its "six parts sum to N" is the sum of
   the **rounded** part values rather than the rounded sum. Confirmed on 11 of 11 split houses.
4. Needs-work score is `round(100 * reno total / ask)`. 0 reads "None found" and 1 to 5 "Light"
   in `--clear`, 6 to 14 "Moderate" in `--warn`, 15 and over "Substantial" in `--flag`.
   **The 15 boundary is inferred and unverifiable**: the batch has nothing between 14 and 30.
5. The table `scorecol` letter is `--accent` for A grades, `--ink` for B, `--muted` for C; its
   bar is `--warn` when the rounded score is under 45 and `--accent` otherwise.
6. Renovation line names capitalise each space-separated word, which is why the page reads
   "Main Bath" and "Tank Removal" but "Oil→gas".
7. `SHOW_DATA` house names are title-cased while `HOUSES` names keep the record's raw case, so
   the same house is "1177 Grand Blvd" in one script and "1177 GRAND Blvd" in the other.

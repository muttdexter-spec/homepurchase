## 1. Is the scoring sound, and what is missing

Take the eight components one at a time. "Sound" means the 0 to 100 mapping measures the thing
a buyer means by the word.

| Component | Rationale as built | Verdict | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Condition | share of this house's work not expected, from the cost model's own probabilities, age-aware mechanicals, photo cap at ~80 | sound | the denominator fix already in the last prompt |
| Baths | count of full plus powder | sound, coarse | none |
| Parking | garage × 3 + spots, cap 8 | sound | none |
| Space | saturates at 1,800 sq ft, caps at 2,600, basement as amenity | shape is right, **the knee is mine** | two buyer numbers (§5) |
| Layout | bedroom count, primary over 150, tiny-room rule | crude: a 4-bed with no ensuite and a chopped main floor scores 100 | add ensuite from the room tables (§5) |
| Lot | 4,000 sq ft scores 0, 9,000 scores 100 | **the 4,000 floor zeros half the batch**, so lot is a bungalow bonus | two buyer numbers (§5) |
| Location | walk + transit + a school within 500 m | **weak**: no commute, no noise, no street type, no pocket; and it is 5% of the score | rebuild (§2) |
| Price | 5-year cost on band anchors $54k to $84k a year | works, but the anchors are the search band, not you | buyer-anchored on monthly payment (§3) |

**The one major factor that is missing is location, and it is missing twice**: the component
measures the wrong things, and at 5% it could not matter even if it measured the right ones.
For a couple buying a first house in Halton and commuting to Toronto, where the house is
(GO access, how loud the street is, whether it is a court or a through road, what pocket it is
in) is normally one of the three things the decision turns on. Rebuild it before the pairwise
elicitation, so the pairs are chosen against a location score that means something.

Everything else that might be added (style preference, main-floor family room, backing onto
green space, orientation, resale pocket) is either a preference to elicit later, in the room
tables (item 1 of the backlog, still not authorised), or unmeasurable without sold data.

## 3. Price, buyer-anchored, and why there is no separate value component

Price should be one component, not two. "Value" is quality per dollar, and quality per dollar is
what the whole score already is once price is one of its terms with your weight on it; a second
value component would count price twice and put a ratio back in the rank, which is how the
$/sq ft sort and the frontier both went wrong.

What is wrong with the current price term is not that it is one term. It is the scale. $54,000 a
year scores 100 and $84,000 scores 0 because those are the ends of the search band, and a linear
scale between them says the first $10,000 above the cheapest house hurts as much as the last
$10,000 below the ceiling. That is not how a buyer feels price. A buyer has a payment that is
comfortable, below which cheaper is nice but not decisive, and a payment that is the most they
will carry, above which the house is out. So:

```
comfortable_mo, max_mo       # two numbers from you, in monthly-payment terms (§5)
price = 100                                   if payment ≤ comfortable_mo
      = 100 · (max_mo − payment) / (max_mo − comfortable_mo)   between
      = 0 and GATED                           if payment > max_mo
```

`payment` is the page's Monthly payment at the `costs.yaml` defaults (P&I + tax + upkeep), the
one "/mo" number, so the component, the calculator and the gate agree by construction. The
5-year cost stays as the honest "what owning costs beyond equity" figure in the money block; it is
not the component, because you feel the payment and the payment is what you told us the ceiling
in. The weight on price still comes from the pairs.

Two consequences to see before accepting. Every house at or under your comfortable payment
scores 100 on price, so among those houses price stops mattering and the other seven decide. And
the anchors are yours, so the score keeps meaning the same thing when the search band moves.

## 4. Why each score, redesigned

The panel is right. The prose under it reads as documentation because it explains the scale
("4,000 sq ft scores 0 and 9,000 scores 100") when the reader is asking where the 68 came from.
Three changes.

**4.1 One stacked bar under the panel: where the 68 comes from.** Eight segments in weight
order, each `weight × score / 100` wide, labelled with the points it contributes; the remainder
to 100 in the light track colour. Under it, one line: "68 = condition 24 · space 18 · layout 11 ·
baths 6 · location 4 · price 3 · parking 2 · lot 1". Then a second line, the actionable one:
"Left on the table: price 12 · condition 10 · space 4 · lot 2 · baths 2 · location 1". That
second line is the why: it says which components are costing this house points and by how much,
in score units, not in dollars or square feet.

**4.2 Per component, one line and a range, not a paragraph.** In the same weight order:

```
Condition   70   ●────────  16 ─── 74 batch     $73k of $240k expected · adds 24 of 34
Space       83   ──────●─   28 ─── 92           2,144 sq ft up, finished basement · adds 18 of 21
Price       18   ●──────    0 ─── 100           $5,728 /mo · adds 3 of 15
```

The range bar is the `.scorecol` mini-bar with two ticks (batch min and max) and a dot (this
house). The fact is ten words. The "adds X of Y" is the contribution. Nothing about how the scale
is built appears here.

**4.3 The scales go to one place.** A "How the scales work" box in the glossary: one line per
component with its 0 and its 100 and where the number comes from. Linked from the panel footnote.
Never repeated on a card.

**4.4 Effective influence, once, in the mast or the weights panel.** Weight times the batch
range, so you can see what actually moves the rank under the current weights: with the
provisional weights, condition 34% over a 58-point range is 20 effective points, price 15% over
100 is 15, lot 3% over 100 is 3. Show it as one row of eight numbers next to the weights, labelled
"what moves the rank".

## 5. Four questions for you, in monthly-payment and square-foot terms

These replace four asserted anchors with your numbers. They are not weights; the pairs do the
weights. Answer them in the prompt.

1. **Payment.** What monthly payment (P&I + tax + upkeep) are you comfortable with, and what is
   the most you will carry? Two numbers. → `price.comfortable_mo`, `price.max_mo`, and the G3
   gate.
2. **Space.** Below how many square feet above grade would a house feel cramped for you, and
   above how many would more space stop mattering? Two numbers. → `space.floor_sqft`,
   `space.knee_sqft` (the cap stays at 2,600).
3. **Yard.** Does a yard matter to you at all? If yes: what lot size is small, and what is
   plenty? → `lot.floor_sqft`, `lot.cap_sqft`; if no, lot is fixed at 50 for every house and
   the pairs will give it a low weight.
4. **Ensuite.** Does a primary ensuite matter? If yes, layout becomes bedrooms 0 to 80, primary
   over 150 +10, ensuite +10, read from the room tables (25 houses) and the bath-by-level fields
   (8); houses with neither carry a `Layout partly estimated` stamp.

## 6. The money block

Five numbers, and the reconciliation that makes two of them make sense together.

```
Monthly payment   $5,728 /mo     $5,176 P&I · $405 tax · $147 upkeep · at 4.25%, 20% down
Cash on closing   $288,241       $239,800 down · $16,455 land transfer · $3,500 closing · $28,486 day-one work
Day-one work      $28,486        p80, before you move in
Wish list         $74,831        p80, kitchen, baths, basement, if you choose
5-year cost       $364,532       payments $344k + closing $20k + commission out $66k + day-one $28k
                                 − equity paid down $131k − day-one recovered $13k. No appreciation assumed.
```

The sub-lines are the point. Cash on closing shows what it is made of, so "mortgage" and "down
payment" need no cells of their own. The 5-year cost sub-line reconciles it to the monthly
payment in one sentence, which is the question the two numbers raised. Remove the "Price and
financing" strip entirely: asking price is in the header line, mortgage and down payment are in
the cash sub-line, land transfer is there too, "hold, a year" is the 5-year cost divided by five,
and "Price 37 / 100 (0 at $84,000, 100 at $54,000)" is the panel's price row with the scale
explanation that §4.3 moves to the glossary.

## 7. Ranking optimisations

1. **Pairs after the rebuild.** Re-select the fifteen pairs after §2, §3 and §5 are in, so the
   choices are made against the components that will be ranked on. If the pairs were already
   answered on the 14 September build, keep those answers, re-fit on the new components, and
   report how much the fit moved.
2. **Fit quality gates the weights.** If the fitted weights reproduce fewer than 11 of 15
   choices for a person, that person's weights stay provisional and the page says "choices were
   not consistent enough to fit; the six they disagree with are …". Inconsistent choices are
   information, not a weight vector.
3. **Ties.** Houses within 1.0 point share a rank ("=4"). The band already covers this; the rank
   should not pretend 62.1 and 61.6 are ordered.
4. **Robust stamp.** A house in the top ten under every weight set on file (alex, partner if
   present, ROC prior, and each with price at 0.5× and 1.5× its weight) gets `Robust`. Cheap
   now that the weights are in a file.
5. **One provisional stamp, not 37.** `Weights provisional` belongs in the mast once. Per-card
   stamps are for facts about the card.
6. **The band** keeps the condition uncertainty from the last prompt, and adds the price
   uncertainty when the rate setting differs from the rank's: rank at 4.25% and at the viewed
   rate.

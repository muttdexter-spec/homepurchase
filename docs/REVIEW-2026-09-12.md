## 0. The short version

1. **v4 is five axes with about two dimensions of information in them.** Three of the five
   (cost efficiency, exposure, carry) are size or price in different clothing, and one of them
   (exposure) is a step function of an ask-derived benchmark. The geometric mean then rewards the
   house with no distinguishing feature. Barberry at #1 is regression to the mean, not a finding.
2. **The right shape is not a composite.** It is gates, one money number over the hold, one
   facts-only fit score, and a dominance frontier between them. "Overpay versus the cheapest
   house at least this good" is the headline, in dollars per month, with the dominating house
   named in the reason line. It renders on a phone card. It needs one preference parameter, not
   five weights, and that parameter cannot be fitted from the 37 rows; it has to be elicited, and
   the frontier itself is the elicitation instrument.
3. **On this data the ranking is essentially "cheapest house within three fit points."** The
   hold-period cost is 98% capital cost on the ask. Day-one work moves it by a standard deviation
   of $84/month; taxes by $63/month. That is the honest result and it means the cost model's job
   is the verdict, the outliers (Enfield, Windermere) and the showing agenda, not the rank.
4. **Three houses are on the frontier under every parameter setting I tried:** Cavendish,
   Clinton, Hazelwood. Who joins them (Bunton or Stillmeadow; Tania or Delaney; Enfield at a
   10-year hold) depends on preferences, which is exactly what should be true.
5. **Plainly wrong in the current code, rank-moving:** `"inish" in basement` matches
   "Un**finish**ed" (Grand rank 17 should be about 27; Crosby 28 should be 37); "Walk-Up" gets
   the walkout weight on three of four listings; "Partially Finished" is estimated as a full
   finished basement (all of Enfield's 3-to-17 band); unknown build year skips the pre-1970
   electrical charge; Enfield is charged a furnace twice; and the stated principle "a house with 40
   immaculate photos is scored identically to a house with no photos" is not what the code does
   (median $13k of day-one cost is removed by clean photo tells, 16 of those houses with a rug or
   mat noted over the floor in the same record).

## 1. Where the v4 proposal is wrong

I computed the five v4 axes on the 37 rows exactly as specified (anchors, clamps, Fit as
`fact_score - size`).

**1a. The axes are not five dimensions.**

```
              cost    fit    exp  carry burden | log(usable)   ask    lot
cost          1.00   0.21   0.78  -0.13  -0.29 |   0.80       -0.28  -0.41
fit           0.21   1.00   0.03  -0.53  -0.30 |   0.43        0.28   0.48
exp           0.78   0.03   1.00  -0.09   0.19 |   0.71        0.04  -0.64
carry        -0.13  -0.53  -0.09   1.00  -0.20 |  -0.47       -0.50  -0.24
burden       -0.29  -0.30   0.19  -0.20   1.00 |  -0.08        0.43  -0.36
eigenvalues of the correlation matrix: 1.93, 1.41, 1.25, 0.33, 0.08
```

Two components carry 67% of the variance, three carry 92%. Cost and exposure are the same
variable (r = 0.78, both r ≈ 0.7 to 0.8 with log usable area). Putting 0.30 + 0.20 = 0.50 of the
weight on them is putting half the weight on size, which is the complaint v4 was written to fix.

**1b. Exposure is a step function of an ask-derived number, not a measurement.** `recovered_pct`
is 23 to 33% for every house where `arv_psf_renovated × usable < ask` (12 houses) and 48 to 67% for
every house where it is above (25 houses). There is no middle. The `conformity.past_norm = 0.45`
haircut on spend beyond the "norm" is what makes it binary, and the norm is `$720 or $790 per
usable sq ft`, derived from asks. The same formula gives Tania a "renovated peer value" of
$2,311,000 (headroom $1.04M on a $1.27M ask) and Weir $1,185,000, below its own asking price.
Both are artifacts. An axis built on this inherits both the size sort and the artifact, and v4's
own note that it "still rests on ask-derived resale values" understates it: it rests on the sign
of one ask-derived number.

**1c. Carry is 83% property tax.** `reserve_monthly` takes four values across all 37 listings
(108, 147, 192, 212), because every house carries the same age-blind reserve. The remaining
variance is `annual_taxes / 12`, which in Halton is a lagged MPAC assessment, i.e. a slow proxy
for price. Anchors of $800 to $480 on that give a 0.15-weight axis that says "prefer the house
that was assessed lower in 2016." Taxes are real money and belong in the cost; they are not an
axis.

**1d. Burden penalises the cheap house that needs work, by construction.** `(day1+wish) /
(ask+day1+wish)` falls as ask rises. Enfield scores 0 not because $294k of work is unusual on a
1954 bungalow but because the ask is $825k. v4 admits "this is a question about the buyer." Then it
should not be an axis with a fixed weight; it should be a parameter the buyer sets (see §3).

**1e. "Absolutely anchored" is not true.** Every anchor (850 to 480 psf, 2 to 10% sunk, 20 to 70
recovered, $800 to $480 carry, 18 to 4% burden) is the observed range of this batch, rounded.
They are batch-relative anchors with an absolute label. The evidence shrink toward the batch
median then adds a second batch dependency, which v4 also notes breaks cross-time comparability.

**1f. The geometric mean plus correlated axes rewards the median house.** A geometric mean of
five axes, three of them the same underlying variable, penalises any house that is unusual on
that variable in either direction, and the shrink then pulls toward the median again. Barberry
(rank 16 today, fit 60.6, $1.199M, 1,979 usable) is the house closest to the middle of the batch on
almost everything. That is why it is #1. It is not wrong to visit Barberry; it is wrong to think
the mean found something.

**1g. Layer 0 is mostly no-ops as written.** "Evidence: low cannot be ranked": no listing is
evidence:low. "True cost above the buyer ceiling", "cash to close above cash available",
"effective bedrooms below requirement": none of the three numbers is defined anywhere in the
package. The gates that would actually bite are unspecified; the one that is specified never fires.

**1h. Layer 2's perturbation understates the one estimate that matters.** Basement area sigma of
12% where `bg_sqft_est` is set: the 20 stated rows give a stated/estimated ratio of 0.54 to 1.46,
and it is not noise, it is style. Sidesplits run 0.64 to 0.79 (the basement is under part of the
footprint); bungalows and two-storeys run 1.10 to 1.46. A 12% sigma around a number with a ±30%
style bias produces a confident wrong band. (Under §3 this stops mattering because estimated
basement area leaves the rank entirely.)

**1i. I could not verify the v4 table or the stability figures.** The proposal's rank table was
"computed with letter grades standing in for the raw fact score" and the P(top 8) numbers came
from code not in the package. My recomputation of the five axes reproduces the qualitative
picture (Barberry, Seton, Ravine high; Enfield low) but not the exact scores.

**Is the whole shape wrong? Yes.** Not because a composite can never work, but because with 37
price-filtered rows and no revealed preference there is no way to set five weights, and with
three of the five axes measuring the same thing the weights do not even mean what they say. The
right shape is in §3.

## 2. Section 6, answered

### 2.1 Is a multi-axis composite the right formulation?

No. For one buyer with one purchase the decision has exactly two kinds of input:

- **Money**, which is measurable: what it costs to buy, own for H years and exit. Price, day-one
  work, taxes, reserve, land transfer tax, financing, commission. All known or well-bounded.
- **Everything else**, which is preference: how much house, layout, lot, location, condition you
  would live with. Not measurable from listings, only from the buyer.

A composite pretends the second is measurable by asserting weights. A pure utility-over-money
formulation pretends the first captures everything, and on this data it would hang the whole
decision on `arv_psf_renovated`, the worst-known number in the model. Stochastic dominance is
the right *idea* (it refuses to assert weights) but on five correlated axes it is toothless (v4
found 19 of 37 non-dominated; I find 12 of 34 even on two axes with zero tolerance).

The formulation that fits the problem is a **lexicographic gate ladder over a two-axis dominance
frontier**:

1. Gates, non-compensatory, buyer-set (STOP conditions, minimum program, cash, budget).
2. Money: hold-period cost of ownership, one number, dollars per month.
3. Fit: the fact grade, facts only, condition can only subtract.
4. Rank on **overpay** = own cost minus the own cost of the cheapest house whose fit is within
   a tolerance of this one. Zero means "on the frontier." Everything else is "you are paying $X/mo
   more than House Y for the same amount of house," which is a sentence.

This is stochastic dominance with a tolerance, on the two axes that are actually independent,
with the money axis carrying all the measurable things and the fit axis carrying all the
preference things. The frontier is the shortlist. Ranking *within* the frontier needs one number,
the buyer's exchange rate between dollars and fit points, and that is the one thing to elicit.

### 2.2 Weights and anchors from 37 rows, no solds, no revealed preference

**You cannot.** The 37 rows are what sellers ask, filtered to a price band. They contain no
information about what you and your fiancée want. Any weight fitted on them is a weight fitted on
sellers. The hedonic fit already told you this (R² = 0.26); I re-ran it with lot area and
municipality and got the same answer (log(above-grade) t = 3.0, Oakville dummy +11.5% t = 3.4,
lot t = 1.4, nothing else).

What the design above does is reduce the number of asserted preference parameters from
five weights plus ten anchors to:

| Parameter | Where it sits | Who sets it | Default I used |
|---|---|---|---|
| space knee (sq ft above grade past which a sq ft is worth little) | fit | buyer | 1,800 |
| condition demote for a confirmed-original kitchen / bath | fit | buyer | 6 to 8 / 4 pts, era-scaled |
| fit tolerance for "about as much house" | dominance | model | 3 pts |
| hold length H | money | buyer | 5 yr (3 / 10 shown) |
| cost of capital, exit commission | money | market | 4.0%, 5.5% |
| minimum program (beds, baths) | gates | buyer | 3 beds AG, 1 full bath |

The market ones are not preferences. The buyer ones are, and §2.9 gives the elicitation.

Do **not** fit anything else. In particular do not try to fit `arv_psf_renovated` or a land
coefficient on this corpus; the range restriction on the dependent variable makes every
coefficient biased toward zero and the sign of the residual is not recoverable.

### 2.3 Marginal square footage for a 3 to 10 year hold, with 18 estimated basements

Three separate things are being conflated by `usable_sqft`:

1. **Above-grade area is stated and is preference.** Value it with a saturating shape anchored
   on your program, not linearly. A couple planning a family gets most of the value of a 3-bed
   house by ~1,800 sq ft above grade; the next 800 sq ft is nice and costs money to heat, tax and
   clean. Linear 1,000 to 2,600 says the 26th hundred sq ft is worth as much as the 11th. That is
   what made Tania #1: 3,210 usable sq ft of which about 1,200 are past the point of use.
2. **Below-grade area is half-estimated and should not be a number in the rank at all.** Treat a
   finished basement as a discrete amenity (+3), partial (+1), with exterior access (+2, suite
   potential; walk-out, walk-up, separate entrance and "Apartment" all qualify). Those flags are
   stated MLS text. The 70% estimate is fine for *costing* a refinish and should stay there.
3. **Land is not in the model and drives the bottom third of the rank.** Every house ranked 25 to
   37 today is a 1950 to 1972 bungalow or split on a 56 to 75 ft lot, asking $140 to $190 per lot
   sq ft against $300 to $450 for the 1996 to 2008 two-storeys on 31 to 40 ft lots. The $/usable
   sort reads them as bad houses; the market is pricing the dirt. For a 3 to 5 year hold this is
   mostly irrelevant to *you* (you are buying a place to live). For a 7 to 10 year hold, land
   holds value and structure depreciates, so lot should carry more fit weight at H = 10. The lot
   component (15 pts, 4,000 to 9,000 sq ft) already does this crudely; a `lot.pts` that rises with
   H is the honest version.

On the hold: the v5 order is nearly invariant to H = 3 / 5 / 10 (§4). The only hold-driven move is
Enfield, which is #7 at five years and #1 at ten (its low ask beats its high day-one work once
capital cost accumulates). That is the correct sensitivity to surface, and it surfaces as one line.

### 2.4 Should wishlist stay out of the ranking?

Out of the **money**, yes. In the **fit**, as a demote-only condition term, also yes. Rationale:

- Two houses at the same price, one with a 2026 kitchen and one with a 1972 kitchen, are not
  the same house. Today they tie. v4 fixes this by putting wishlist dollars in a burden axis,
  which drags the ask back in.
- A confirmed-original kitchen costs you one of two things: living with it (a fit cost) or
  ~$50k of which the AIC bands say you get 75 to 100% back (a sunk cost of ~$10k). The fit
  framing is the larger, more conservative penalty, uses no ask-derived number, and keeps
  "photos demote, never promote" in the grade where you want it.
- Scale the demote by era. A 2008 builder kitchen with a topmount sink (Columbus) is not a
  1965 kitchen with a topmount sink. The tells mean different things in different decades; the
  cost model already knows this (`kitchen_cosmetic` vs `kitchen_full`), the grade did not.

Keep `wish_p80` on the card as information. The buyer decides timing.

### 2.5 Uncertainty: P(top 8) or something legible?

Not P(top 8). It hard-codes 8, it reports a probability of a rank threshold rather than a
range, and on v4's correlated axes it produces false confidence (Barberry 98%).

Rank the p80 point, and show the **rank band from re-ranking inside the model's own day-one
draws** (v5 does this: 1,000 re-rankings, p10 to p90). Then use it asymmetrically:

- **Visit if the band's top end reaches the shortlist**, even if the point rank does not.
  Visits are cheap relative to the decision. Resolve uncertainty by looking, not by shrinking.
- **Offer on the pessimistic end** (the p80 you already use).

On this data the bands are honest and informative: the C+ tier (Cavendish, Enfield, Caplan,
Bonnie) is a four-way tie whose frontier member changes with the draw (band 1 to 12 for each);
Ravine (2 to 3) and Hazelwood (4 to 8) are solid. That tells you something a point rank cannot.

### 2.6 Evidence: shrink, gate, or display?

**Display, and widen the band. Never shrink.** Shrinking toward the batch median moves a
low-evidence house into the middle, where it neither gets visited nor dismissed. The cost model
already handles thin evidence correctly: unobserved tells stay at the era prior, which is a wider
day-one distribution, which is a wider rank band, which (per 2.5) makes the house *more* likely to
be visited. That is the right direction. The stamp stays on the card. `evidence: low` blocking
"See first" is fine as a rule; it is a no-op today.

### 2.7 "Photos demote, never promote"

Keep it, but state it precisely, because the code does not do what the brief says and the code
is closer to right.

The brief says a house with 40 immaculate photos is scored identically to one with no photos.
It is not. `floor_condition = no_defect_seen` (36 of 37) and `ceiling_main = flat_painted` (34
of 37) drop those day-one lines to p = 0.15 from an era prior of up to 0.70. Setting every
clean tell back to `not_shown` raises `day1_p80` by a median of $13,272 per house (Delaney
+$23.8k, Tania +$22.6k; Enfield +$3.6k because it already had nothing clean). 16 of the 36 with a
clean floor read carry a `concealed_surfaces` note about that same floor (rugs, gym mats,
runners), and the note is not wired to anything.

The Overton failure was a *surface* misread at contact-sheet resolution, caught by full-res
tells (topmount sink, laminate edge). So the principle that actually protects you is:

> A full-resolution read of a **finish surface** may lower that line's probability toward the
> floor, never to zero, and never when the surface is noted as concealed. Photographs may
> **never** lower the probability of a **structure, envelope or mechanical** line (panel, wiring,
> plumbing, waterproofing, roof, furnace, A/C, windows), and may never raise the fact grade.

The code already obeys the second sentence. It violates the first on concealed floors. Fix in §6.

### 2.8 Presentation: one number and a reason on a phone

The v5 headline is **overpay per month**, with the dominating house named:

```
$0 /mo          Frontier · nothing within 3 pts of fit 68.3 is cheaper
+$153 /mo       vs Hazelwood (A-, $1,235,000) for about the same house
+$1,781 /mo     vs Cavendish (C+, $969,900) for about the same house · HOLD: tenanted
```

Underneath, unchanged: ask, day-one cash, own/mo, grade, band. The frontier houses are shown as
a set ("5 houses on the frontier, pick your price point") rather than ranks 1 to 5, because their
order among themselves is the preference you have not stated yet. This is strictly more legible
standing in a house than `$620 per usable sq ft`, which needs the batch in your head to mean
anything.

### 2.9 Elicitation that would fix the weights

All of it can be done with the listings you already have, in about fifteen answers:

1. **The frontier question (one answer, sets the exchange rate).** "Cavendish C+ at $5,200/mo,
   Bunton B− at $5,690, Ravine B− at $5,900, Clinton B+ at $6,270, Hazelwood A− at $6,575. At
   these numbers, which one?" Right now the frontier's steps cost $115, $48, $42 and $46 per fit
   point per month (Cavendish→Bunton→Ravine→Clinton→Hazelwood). The first step is the steepest,
   so a *linear* exchange rate only ever lands on Cavendish (below $57/pt/mo) or Hazelwood
   (above). If you pick Bunton, Ravine or Clinton, you have told the model your preference for
   fit is not linear (getting to B+ matters a lot, A− only a little more), which is a legitimate
   answer and exactly why this is asked as a choice among five houses and not as a number.
2. **Six near-frontier pairs (tests whether fit is missing something).** Barberry vs Clinton
   (+$12/mo, Oakville vs Burlington). Delaney vs Hazelwood (+$153, stated basement and lower
   day-one vs higher taxes). Stillmeadow vs Bunton (+$207, Oakville). Tania vs Delaney (+$128,
   3,200 sq ft vs 2,560). Seton vs Ravine (+$385, 2026 full reno vs pool). Enfield vs Cavendish
   (+$43, 9,154 sq ft lot and oil vs walk-up basement). If you consistently pick the "dominated"
   house, the fit score is missing a term (municipality is the obvious candidate: the asks say
   Oakville carries +11.5% for the same house; only you know whether it is worth that to you).
3. **After each showing, two numbers:** would you pay ask (yes/no), and what would you pay.
   Eight showings gives a willingness-to-pay series that can replace the fit score's weights
   with a fitted one. This is the only revealed preference that exists and the showing record
   already has the fields to hold it.

Data acquisitions worth the cost, in order: (a) sold $/sq ft by pocket from the agent, which
replaces `arv_psf_renovated` and makes resale a real number; (b) distance to the nearest GO
station for each address, computable from the address, which is the location variable a
Toronto commuter in Burlington/Oakville actually has and the walk/transit scores do not carry;
(c) mechanical ages, already a standing question, now with 18 seller claims to verify rather
than ask open.

## 3. The design (specification)

Everything below is implemented in `pipeline/rank_v5.py`, which imports `score.py` unchanged.

### 3.1 Gates (non-compensatory, in order)

```
G0  STOP           verdict() == STOP                          -> excluded, shown at bottom
G1  program        beds_effective < min_beds_ag (3)           -> excluded, reason shown
                   baths_full     < min_baths_full (1)
G2  budget         ask + day1_p80 > max_all_in                -> excluded  (None = off)
G3  cash           cash_to_close_20pct > max_cash_to_close    -> excluded  (None = off)
H   HOLD flags     oil tank / in-ground pool / tenanted       -> ranked, flagged, not "See first"
```

Today G1 removes Kent (2 beds above grade) and Middlesmoor (a 41 sq ft "bedroom" in the room
table drops it to 2 effective; verify, see §5.9). G2/G3 are yours to set; the search's price
filter is currently doing G2's job.

### 3.2 Money: hold-period cost of ownership

Under zero appreciation and "you get the price back at exit," which is the neutral assumption
when you do not trust resale:

```
own_H = (1 - rec_day1) * day1_p80                      # sunk part of day-one work
      + (r * H) * (ask + day1_p80) + comm * ask        # financing + opportunity + exit commission
      + H * annual_taxes
      + H * 12 * reserve_monthly
      + LTT_net(ask) + closing_extras
own_mo = own_H / (12 * H)
```

- `rec_day1`: expected-spend-weighted AIC mid recovery over the day-one lines, **no conformity
  cap** (the cap is the ask-derived part). Typically 0.45 to 0.55.
- `r = 0.040` blended cost of capital (mortgage on 80%, opportunity on the 20%), `comm = 0.055`
  (5% + HST). Market numbers, not preferences; set once in `costs.yaml`.
- `H` is the buyer's toggle. Ranked at 3, 5 and 10.
- Appreciation is deliberately zero: it is common to every house in Halton, so it cannot rank
  them, and its idiosyncratic part (renovation recovery) is small and badly measured.

Composition on the 37 rows: capital $5,086/mo mean (sd $510), taxes $461 (sd $63), closing $323
(sd $41), day-one sunk $256 (sd $84), reserve $152 (sd $15). Correlation of capital with the
total: 0.98. **This is the finding**: once you stop dividing by square feet, the money side is the
ask, and the cost model differentiates only at the tails.

### 3.3 Fit: facts, absolute, condition demote-only

Same component maxima as v3 (25 / 20 / 20 / 15 / 12 / 8 = 100) so bands stay comparable.

```
space (25)   16 pts linear 1,000 -> knee (1,800) sq ft above grade
             + 4 pts linear knee -> 2,600
             + 3 finished basement, or +1 partially finished        (MLS text, not estimate)
             + 2 exterior access: Separate Entrance / Walk-Out / Walk-Up / Apartment
layout (20)  unchanged (effective beds, tiny-bed rule, primary >= 150)
baths  (20)  unchanged
lot    (15)  unchanged (raise pts with H; see 2.3)
location(12) unchanged (GO distance is the missing input)
parking (8)  unchanged
split dock   0.94 unchanged (it is a preference; elicit it)
condition    -8 kitchen confirmed full gut, -6 confirmed cosmetic, -4 main bath confirmed
             original, -2 secondary bath confirmed original, cap -12,
             x era_scale {pre_1990: 1.0, 1990_2004: 0.67, 2005_plus: 0.33}
             "Confirmed" = the cost model's p >= 0.95 on that line. not_shown => 0. Clean => 0.
```

### 3.4 Rank

```
dominated_by(i) = argmin own_H over { j : fit_j >= fit_i - tol }     tol = 3 pts
overpay(i)      = own_H(i) - own_H(dominated_by(i))                   0 => frontier
sort by (overpay, own_H)
band            = p10..p90 of the rank over 1,000 re-rankings, each using one day-one draw
                  per house from the existing seeded simulation
```

The frontier is displayed as a set. Within it there is no rank until the exchange rate is
elicited (§2.9). If you must have one before then, `own_mo - lambda * fit` with lambda = the
end-to-end slope of the frontier ($57/pt/mo today) is indifferent between Cavendish and
Hazelwood and never selects the three houses between them, because the frontier's first step
is its steepest. Treat any such number as a placeholder; the choice-among-five question in
§2.9 is the real instrument.

### 3.5 What stays exactly as it is

`cost()`, `resale()` (for display), `verdict()`, `evidence()`, `cash_to_close()`, the simulation,
the showing record and its in-person promotion. The card keeps every section it has. Only the
headline, the sort, and the fact grade's space and condition terms change.

## 5. Plainly wrong in the cost model and the code

Ordered by effect on the current rank.

**5.1 `"inish" in basement` matches "Unfinished"** (`score.py:38`, also `evidence():325`). Grand
("Full, Unfinished") gets 800 sq ft of estimated *finished* basement and Crosby 562. Usable
2,115 → 1,715 and 1,486 → 1,205; all-in psf 630 → 776 and 796 → 981; v3 rank 17 → about 27 and
28 → 37. Both also get a p = 0.70 wishlist "refinish" line instead of a p = 1.0-if-wanted "finish"
line, and the evidence stamp says "basement area estimated" when the correct statement is "no
finished basement."

**5.2 "Partially Finished" is estimated as a full finished basement** (`score.py:38`). Enfield
gets 906 sq ft (capped to 900 for costing) on a "Partially Finished" string, which is its entire
3-to-17 rank band. Pallatine has a stated area so escapes. Suggest factor 0.35 for partial.

**5.3 `"alk" in basement` matches "Walk-Up"** (`score.py:217`). Cavendish, Osborne and Weir get
the 0.65 walkout weight; only Windermere is a walk-out. Cavendish's v3 rank 9 becomes 16 at
0.50 ($620/sf).

**5.4 Photos promote through the day-one lines, and concealed floors are not wired.** Detail in
§2.7. Median $13.3k of `day1_p80` per house; 16 of 36 clean floor reads have a rug/mat note on the
same floor in `concealed_surfaces`. `floor_condition` has no discriminating power (36/37 the same
value) and is the second-largest day-one line.

**5.5 Unknown build year skips the pre-1970 electrical block** (`score.py:165`). Caplan and
Aldridge (year unknown, Burlington raised bungalow and sidesplit) are treated as pre-1990 for
`p_not_done`, pre-1980 for contingency, and post-1970 for panel and knob-and-tube because
`if yb and yb < 1970` is false for `None`. Unknown is worst-case everywhere except the one place
it is most expensive. Expected omission about $4k plus contingency each.

**5.6 Enfield pays for a furnace twice.** `oil_to_gas` ("furnace + line to street + decommission")
at p = 1.0 in day-one, plus the `furnace` reserve at p = 1.0. Zero the furnace reserve when
oil→gas fires.

**5.7 The reserve bucket has no remaining-life concept.** Wyandotte's windows are
`aluminum_original CONFIRMED, near term` (the code's own basis string), p = 1.0, and are annualised at 1/30 ($64/mo) as if new.
`near_term_5yr` computes the right thing ($55.9k for Wyandotte) and is written to `detail.json`
but appears nowhere. For a hold, confirmed-original components belong at (cost / min(H,
remaining life)), not (cost / life).

**5.8 The resale stack produces artifacts at both ends.** `norm = psf × usable` gives Tania a
renovated peer value of $2,311,000 and Weir $1,185,000 (below ask). `recovered_pct` is 23 to 33%
for the 12 houses where norm < ask and 48 to 67% for the rest, with nothing in between. The
`resale_after_reno` column tells the buyer of Weir that after spending $107k the house is worth
$115k less than they paid, which nobody believes. Until sold data exists: hide `norm`,
`headroom`, `arv_*`, `recovered_pct` and `breakeven`; show only `sunk_if_done = spend × (1 −
AIC mid)`, which is sourced and ask-free.

**5.9 Middlesmoor's 41 sq ft "bedroom"** (`rooms_raw`: `['Second','Bedroom',41]`). 5 ft × 8 ft is
a closet. The tiny-bed rule makes it a 2-bed, layout 2/20, grade C−, and v5 gates it. If it is a
transcription error (141?) it is a 3-bed B− at $1.05M and enters the rank near Bunton. One look at
the listing settles it.

**5.10 `mech_ages_stated` exists for 18 listings and reaches the agent questions and the card,
but not the model.** Correct under the principle (it is remarks text), but the showing record has
`kitchen_seen` / `bath_seen` / `basement_seen` promotion and no `furnace_seen` / `roof_seen` /
`ac_seen` / `panel_seen` equivalents wired into `cost()`, so a verified 2022 furnace can never
leave the reserve. Add them; `furnace_seen` and `water_heater_seen` already appear as fields.

**5.11 Contingency is applied to wishlist** (`score.py:198`) while `costs.yaml:58` says "day1
work only." Doc/code mismatch; the code is probably right.

**5.12 `multi_room_premium` fires on line items, not rooms.** Flooring, ceilings, waterproofing
and basement count as "rooms," so the +8% is on in 77% of Delaney's draws and 100% of Enfield's.
Not wrong, but not "only where real."

**5.13 `test_model.py` does not test the thing it says.** The "photos never promote" check is
`p >= 0.0`. Invariants that would have caught the above:


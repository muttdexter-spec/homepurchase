Eleven houses, ranked by what they actually cost rather than what they ask. Day-one cash
What has to be spent before or at move-in. Safety, insurability, and work that costs twice as much once the furniture is in.
True cost
Asking price plus day-one cash. Not the asking price plus everything you might ever want to do.
Reserve / month
The monthly set-aside for the roof, furnace, A/C, water heater and windows. Not a purchase cost, a running one. On the cards it is shown added to property tax.
Wish list
Kitchen, baths, basement, deck. Your choice, your timing. Deliberately kept out of the ranking.

One rule behind all of this: photos can only ever count against a house, never for it. A kitchen that looks immaculate in forty photos scores exactly the same as a kitchen with no photos at all. So the green column is only ever "no problem found", and the red column is the real agenda for the visit.

Appraisal Institute of Canada bands, with the bathroom low end pulled down to match the RE/MAX renovation report. Almost nothing returns more than it costs. Spending past what a renovated house in that pocket sells for returns 45 cents on the dollar, which is the arithmetic behind the old rule about not over-improving for the street.

The single most sensitive number on this page: a renovated house is assumed to trade at $720 per usable sq ft in Burlington and $790 in Oakville. Both are derived from asking prices, not sold prices. Move them by $60 and several of the resale figures move by six digits. Ask the agent for sold price per square foot in each pocket, and this stops being a guess.

Two questions that go to every listing agent, always

  1. How old are the roof, furnace and air conditioner?
  2. Are any of the photos virtually staged or digitally enhanced, and when were they taken?

Not one of these 24 listings contains a single photograph of an electrical panel, a furnace or a water heater. Across houses built between 1954 and 1999, that is the largest gap in everything above, and the first question closes most of it.

This cannot tell you a house is good

It is a screening tool and a list of things to check. Every green line means "no problem found in the photos", never "verified fine".

None of these are offer prices

There are no sold comparables in this model. Resale estimates rest on an asking-price benchmark and will move the moment real sold data exists.

The calculator is arithmetic, not advice

Rates, insurance premiums and rebate rules change. Confirm every figure with your mortgage broker and your real estate lawyer before you rely on it.

It knows nothing about living there

The street, the light, the noise, the walk to the car in February. All of it outranks everything on this page.
